RETAIL & CRAFT CATALOG

Things made here that you cannot buy anywhere else.

These are not souvenirs manufactured for tourists. They are products that exist because this city has sustained them — some for over a hundred years. You will not find them in Mumbai or Pune with the same provenance or freshness.

Researched on the ground in Old Nashik, Tambat Ali & Yeola

Buyer's Intel

The Three Golden Sourcing Rules

1. Kondaji from Bhadrakali: Franchise boxes on Amazon sit for weeks. Buy fresh from the original 1920 Bhadrakali outlet.
2. Copper by weight: In Tambat Ali, pure hand-hammered copper vessels are sold by weight plus artisan making charge.
3. Check cellar-only labels: Sula and Vallonné reserve limited micro-batches that never reach retail liquor stores.

Sula — Estate-Exclusive Bottles

Maker / Provenance Gangapur & Dindori Valleys
Price / Unit ₹800 – ₹3,500 / bottle
Transit / Shelf Life Glass Bottles · Pack in Check-In
Primary Market Sula Estate Shop, Gangapur Rd

The Sula wines you find in Mumbai or Goa have all been through distribution. The bottles sold at the estate shop in Nashik include labels that are never distributed — single-vineyard wines, reserve editions, and the seasonal Riesling that Sula produces in limited quantity from a specific block of vines on the property. The price gap versus retail is significant enough to justify buying several bottles. The estate shop is inside the Sula property; you don't need a tasting ticket to buy.

Since 1920 · The City's Defining Snack HERITAGE

Chitkis, Chivda & Shengdana Chutney

Maker / Provenance Bhadrakali Market · Wavare Bandhu
Price / Unit ₹180 – ₹400 / kg
Transit / Shelf Life 45 Days Shelf Life · Excellent
Primary Market Saraf Bazaar dry goods shops

The holy trinity of Nashik take-home food. Chitkis are sesame-and-jaggery brittle — thin, crisp, intensely sweet. Chivda is spiced flattened rice mixed with peanuts, curry leaves, dried fruit, and mustard — made slightly differently by every family and every shop. Shengdana chutney is dry peanut-spice powder that goes on everything and lasts for months in a jar. All three are made locally, sold cheaply in the dry goods shops around Saraf Bazaar. Get there in the morning when the batches are fresh.

Nashik Spices — Cardamom, Cloves, Dry Chilli

Maker / Provenance Saraf Bazaar · Old City
Price / Unit Market Bullion Rates
Transit / Shelf Life Pouch · Highly Portable
Primary Market Saraf Bazaar

Nashik is a significant agricultural region and its spice markets are well-stocked with high-quality produce. The Saraf Bazaar spice shops sell cardamom, cloves, cinnamon, and dried red chillis at prices well below Mumbai retail. The Nashik region grows a particular variety of dried chilli that's sweeter and more aromatic than the typical Byadgi — worth buying a stock if you cook.

Lesser Known EDIBLES

Raw Sabudana (Sago Pearls)

Maker / Provenance Nashik Market
Price / Unit Moderate
Transit / Shelf Life Easy to Pack
Primary Market CBS area wholesale market

The whole region around Nashik is a major sabudana-processing centre — cassava grown in the surrounding districts is processed into the tapioca pearls used across Maharashtra for sabudana khichdi and sabudana vada. The sabudana bought in Nashik is fresher, cheaper, and more consistently sized than packaged varieties in cities. Dry goods shops near the wholesale markets at CBS.

Tilgul Ladoos — Around Makar Sankranti

Maker / Provenance Main Road & Old City Halwai
Price / Unit ₹400 – ₹700 / kg
Transit / Shelf Life 3 – 5 Days · Consume Fresh
Primary Market Old city mithai shops

Sesame-and-jaggery balls made in winter for Makar Sankranti (mid-January). The tilgul sold in Nashik's mithai shops is made by the same families who have been making it for generations. The ratio of sesame to jaggery is more precise here than you'd find in mass-produced versions — crisp on the outside, chewy inside, with a genuine nuttiness. Available only around Sankranti; extraordinary if you're in town.

Genuinely Local Art CRAFT

Warli Art — Tribal Paintings from the Sahyadri Belt

Maker / Provenance Yeola Handloom Weavers & Saraf Bazar
Price / Unit ₹7,000 – ₹50,000+
Transit / Shelf Life Silk Fold · Very Easy
Primary Market Panchavati Market

Warli is one of India's oldest and most distinctive tribal art forms — white geometric patterns (human figures, animals, trees, ritual scenes) painted on dark mud backgrounds, originating from the Warli tribe of the Nashik-Palghar belt. Authentic Warli paintings are made on cloth, paper, or mud-coated walls by tribal artists from the region. The most authentic versions are sold in the Panchavati Market and through tribal craft exhibitions held periodically in the city. Avoid the mass-produced versions on cheap canvas sold near tourist spots — look for the ones that feel slightly uneven, which means a human hand made them.

Brass & Copper Puja Items — Near the Ghats

Maker / Provenance Tambat Ali · Old Nashik Ghats
Price / Unit ₹500 – ₹3,500 by weight
Transit / Shelf Life Heavy Brass · Indestructible
Primary Market Panchavati approach streets

The shops lining the approach to the Panchavati ghats sell brass diyas, incense holders, copper vessels, and temple bells. Much of it is mass-produced, but mixed in are items made by local craftspeople who have been working brass for generations. A small brass diya or a well-made copper lota (water vessel) is one of the more honest souvenirs you can take from Nashik.

Tibetan Woolens — The Seasonal Market

Maker / Provenance Nashik Market
Price / Unit Moderate
Transit / Shelf Life Easy to Pack
Primary Market Anandvan Colony, Oct–Feb

Every winter, Tibetan traders set up a market (near Anandvan Colony, Nashik) selling handmade woolens — thick mufflers, pashmina-blend shawls, woolen socks, hand-knitted gloves, fleece jackets. The quality is significantly higher than the price suggests. These are genuinely handmade items by Tibetan artisans, not factory-produced imports. The market runs October to February and is worth visiting purely for the warm and unexpected hospitality.

Nashik Specialty EDIBLES

Kolhapuri Chappals — Genuine Versions

Maker / Provenance Nashik Market
Price / Unit Moderate
Transit / Shelf Life Easy to Pack
Primary Market Old city shoe markets

Nashik has a strong Chambar (cobbler caste) community with a long tradition of leather craftsmanship — historically significant enough that Ambedkar organised them at the 1931 Nashik conference. Kolhapuri-style chappals — hand-stitched, vegetable-tanned leather with the characteristic open-toe design — are available at several shops near the old city. The genuine ones are heavier, stiffer when new, and last for years. They are not the thin decorative versions; they are working shoes.

Nashik Specialty JEWELLERY

Saraf Bazaar — Gold & Traditional Maharashtrian Jewellery

Maker / Provenance Saraf Bazaar · Old City
Price / Unit Market Bullion Rates
Transit / Shelf Life Pouch · Highly Portable
Primary Market Saraf Bazaar

Nashik is legitimately known for gold. Saraf Bazaar's concentrated cluster of goldsmiths means competitive pricing and wide selection — everything from traditional Maharashtrian pieces (nath nose rings, tanmani necklaces, bajuband armlets, payal anklets) to contemporary designs. The market rates are visible and honest; bargaining is expected on making charges, not on gold rate itself. For traditional Maharashtrian wedding jewellery in particular, Saraf Bazaar offers a quality and selection that is hard to match outside of Pune.

Lac Bangles — Old City Craftspeople

Maker / Provenance Saraf Bazaar · Old City
Price / Unit Market Bullion Rates
Transit / Shelf Life Pouch · Highly Portable
Primary Market Small lanes off Saraf Bazaar

Nashik has a small tradition of lac bangle-making — the colourful bangles made from shellac resin, often inlaid with glass or mirror chips, that predate plastic jewellery by centuries. A few old-city artisan families still make them. Look in the smaller lanes off Saraf Bazaar. ₹20–₹80 per bangle, depending on work. Fragile, beautiful, and almost impossible to find in the same quality at a mall.

Nashik Specialty CRAFT

Silk Sarees & Paithani — Bombay Silk Stores

Maker / Provenance Yeola Handloom Weavers & Saraf Bazar
Price / Unit ₹7,000 – ₹50,000+
Transit / Shelf Life Silk Fold · Very Easy
Primary Market Bombay Silk Stores, MG Road

Nashik has several reputable silk saree stores that carry Paithani sarees — Maharashtra's most iconic handloom, woven in Paithan (near Aurangabad) with silk and zari, known for the peacock and lotus motifs. The Bombay Silk Stores on MG Road carry good selections. Prices for genuine Paithani start at ₹3,000 and rise sharply for the intricately worked versions. Ask specifically about the warp and weft before buying — genuine Paithani has a distinctive double-woven border.

Actually Useful CRAFT

A Single Brass Diya from Panchavati

Maker / Provenance Tambat Ali · Old Nashik Ghats
Price / Unit ₹500 – ₹3,500 by weight
Transit / Shelf Life Heavy Brass · Indestructible
Primary Market Panchavati market lanes

There are thousands of diyas for sale near the ghats. Most are mass-produced in Rajasthan. A few shops sell locally cast brass diyas of noticeably different weight and finish. The price difference is small; the quality difference is significant. Light one on a Tuesday evening, put a cotton wick in it, and you've brought home something that has functioned the same way on these ghats for two thousand years.

Unexpected HERITAGE

A Zeolite Crystal from Gargoti Museum Shop

Maker / Provenance Gargoti Museum Shop
Price / Unit ₹200 – ₹10,000
Transit / Shelf Life Bubble Wrap Required
Primary Market Gargoti Museum, Sinnar (30km)

The Gargoti Museum in Sinnar sells small mineral specimens from the Deccan Trap basalts — the same geological formations under Nashik. Apophyllite, stilbite, and heulandite crystals that formed in volcanic rock over 65 million years ago, available for ₹100–₹500. Genuinely beautiful, scientifically interesting, and available literally nowhere else. The museum shop is attached to the museum; you don't need to visit the full museum to buy.

Since 1920 · The City's Defining Snack HERITAGE

Kondaji Chivda — Kanda Lasun Chivda

Maker / Provenance Bhadrakali Market · Wavare Bandhu
Price / Unit ₹180 – ₹400 / kg
Transit / Shelf Life 45 Days Shelf Life · Excellent
Primary Market Bhadrakali Market (main outlet)

In 1920, Kondaji Gunaji Wavare quit his job in the British Government and began selling masala poha chivda door-to-door from a cane basket, asking customers to taste it first and pay only if they liked it. A hundred years later, the brand is as synonymous with Nashik as its vineyards. The signature product is the Kanda Lasun Chivda — spiced flattened rice with fried onion, garlic, groundnuts, coconut, cocum, and a masala whose exact composition belongs to the family. The celebrated Marathi writer P.L. Deshpande apparently told a friend in London that no snack anywhere in the world could compare to it. Wavre Bandhu now has 9 retail outlets in Nashik and 72 franchise outlets across the city. The original outlet is at Bhadrakali Market. Also available: Makhmal Chivda (softer, less spiced), Jain Chivda (no onion-garlic), Corn Chivda, and Bhel mixes. Buy from the Bhadrakali outlet for freshest stock. Available on Amazon but the fresh version is categorically better.

Invented Here · Rarely Found Outside Nashik SWEETS

Khurchan Wadi — Mangesh Mithai, Main Road

Maker / Provenance Main Road & Old City Halwai
Price / Unit ₹400 – ₹700 / kg
Transit / Shelf Life 3 – 5 Days · Consume Fresh
Primary Market Mangesh Mithai, Main Road

A halwaai from Mount Abu arrived in Nashik in 1840 and set up what became known as Langda Halwaai. His son — trying to avoid wasting leftover sooji, coconut, and mawa — invented the khurchan wadi: a rich, crumbly sweet that hardened into small squares, unlike anything else in Maharashtra's mithai tradition. The Incredible India food guide describes it as "a sweet that is rarely found outside of Nashik even today." Main Road, old Nashik.

Seasonal · Diwali & Winter SWEETS

Anaarsa & Dinka Laddoos — Bhagwantrao Mithai

Maker / Provenance Main Road & Old City Halwai
Price / Unit ₹400 – ₹700 / kg
Transit / Shelf Life 3 – 5 Days · Consume Fresh
Primary Market Bhagwantrao Mithai, Main Road

Bhagwantrao Mithai on Main Road, founded in 1912 (and credited with making the first-ever misal in Nashik), is famous for its Diwali sweets: anaarsa — a crunchy, honeyed fritter made from fermented rice flour, rarely made outside Maharashtra — and dinka laddoos, made from tree resin (dinka/pine resin), a distinctly Maharashtrian confection that has no equivalent elsewhere. Available around October–November. The shop also sells chakli made from traditional recipes.

Est. 1864 · Original Branch Demolished 2025 SWEETS

Pande Mithai — Traditional Pedhas, Basundi & Raghoda Laddoos

Maker / Provenance Main Road & Old City Halwai
Price / Unit ₹400 – ₹700 / kg
Transit / Shelf Life 3 – 5 Days · Consume Fresh
Primary Market 6 outlets across Nashik (original branch demolished)

Founded by Jagannath Shivlal Pande in 1864 — six generations of the family have run this shop. Famous for traditional pedhas, large-format gulab jamuns, basundi, raghoda laddoos, malai barfi, and the khurchand wadi. The original Main Road branch was demolished in 2025 for Kumbh Mela infrastructure works, which was described as a loss of heritage by Nashikars. The family still runs six other outlets across the city. Buy their sweets — it is an act of continuity with 161 years of Nashik's culinary identity.

Consumer Intelligence

The Honest Souvenir Rule: What's Worth Packing

Not everything sold around temple complexes or expressway tourist plazas is worth your luggage space. Here is the unvarnished breakdown.

WORTH BUYING

Kondaji Kanda Lasun Chivda

From the original 1920 Bhadrakali outlet. The spice blend of onion, garlic, coconut, and kokum has a depth that manufactured factory chivdas cannot replicate.

WORTH BUYING

Hand-Hammered Copper Handa

Direct from Tambat Ali. Heavy, unpolished, hammered entirely by hand. Naturally antimicrobial and will last for generations. Sold by weight plus labor.

SKIP OR BE CAREFUL

Powerloom Paithani Sarees

Shops around roadside tourist junctions often sell machine-made synthetic polyester sarees as "Pure Yeola Paithani". Real Paithani is woven on handlooms with pure silk and real zari, taking 2 to 6 months to craft.
